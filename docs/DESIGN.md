# Design decisions and assumptions

## Design

- **Idea:** one day on Lake Zug, looking south from Zug's lakefront: the Zugerberg on the left, the Rigi, the Bernese Alps low on the horizon, Pilatus on the right. The page is a single scroll, and the scroll is the clock. The visitor arrives before dawn, the sun rises behind the Zugerberg, crosses the range and sets just right of Pilatus, and the page ends at night with lights along the shores and the moon over the Zugerberg. The view turns slowly west with the day, more on a phone than on a wide screen.
- **The scene:** a full-screen canvas behind the page (`src/scripts/scene.js`). It draws the sky, stars, moon, sun, a few thin clouds, five layers of ridges with atmospheric haze and snow on the high Alps, conifer forest on the near slopes, houses along the shores, lights in the summit hotels of the Rigi and Pilatus, a lake steamer, and the lake, which mirrors everything above it in rows that ripple. Each section sets the hours it covers (`data-hours="7.2 8.2"`), and the time is read at the middle of the viewport, so the light always matches where you are.
- **Real terrain:** the skyline is not invented. `tools/terrain/` samples open elevation data along lines of sight from the lakefront by the old town (47.1658 N, 8.5128 E) and records the highest visible angle in each direction, in five distance bands. That gives the true outline of the view south: the Zugerberg's flank, the Rigi (12 km), Pilatus (29 km) and the Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau (76 to 82 km), with the Stanserhorn and Bürgenstock in front of them. It was checked against photographs of the view. The same data draws the top edge of every paper panel (the range from the Rigi to Pilatus); the contour map in the Principles section (40 m interval) is the Zugerberg itself.
- **Light:** the colours by hour follow photographs of this view: a deep blue spring morning with white snow on the Alps, a peach dusk with lavender mountains and a silver lake, and an orange sunset beside Pilatus. A lake steamer crosses from Zug towards Cham as you scroll. A small readout in the corner shows the bearing of the view and the time of day. It steps aside while a paper panel passes beneath it.
- **Windows and sheets:** the page alternates between windows, where the scene shows through and text sits directly on the landscape, and sheets, paper panels whose top edge is the outline of the range. Two short statements stand alone in the sky between sheets and hold their place while the window passes.
- **Location:** the text comes first; then the page opens onto the evening view and names what you see (Zugerberg, Rigi, Eiger, Mönch and Jungfrau, Pilatus, Lake Zug) with thin brass leaders. The names are drawn on the canvas, with the same list in the page for screen readers.
- **Motion:** headings rise word by word out of a mask, the hero wordmark surfaces from the dark, the contour lines draw themselves in as you pass, and buttons fill from below like light coming up a slope. With `prefers-reduced-motion` all of that is off: no ripples, twinkling, parallax, reveals or drawing. The time of day still follows the scroll, because that is information, not decoration.
- **Legibility:** text set directly on the scene has a soft wash behind it (dusk behind light text, morning haze behind dark), so it stays readable at any hour without boxing it in. Contrast was measured against the actual rendered scene behind each line, not only against the page colour.
- **No library:** the X thread of animation tools was a useful benchmark for ambition, but each of those tools solves a different problem (video rendering, 3D, component animation). What this page needs is one bespoke illustration that responds to scroll, which a few hundred lines of Canvas 2D do with no dependency, no WebGL, and a script of 17 KB gzipped for everything on the page.
- **Palette** (CSS variables in `src/styles/tokens.css`): night navy, warm paper `#f4f0e8`, mist blue-greys and muted brass as the only accent. The scene's colours are keyframes by hour in `scene.js`.
- **Typography:** Fraunces (variable, optical sizing) for headings at a light weight, Inter for body text, both self-hosted (Latin subset). Metric-adjusted fallbacks avoid layout shift.
- **Wordmark:** "Alfine" set in Fraunces and converted to outlines, paired with a small line mark of two peaks over a lake.
- **Without JavaScript:** a still SVG drawing of the same skyline at dusk stands in for the scene, and every section, the menu links, the legal texts and the form still work.

## Technical

- **Plain HTML, CSS and vanilla JS** with a ~150-line Node build script and no dependencies. The skyline data is inlined into the script at build time. This keeps the build fast, the output fully static and the supply chain empty. Copy lives in one content file per language; templates are small JavaScript functions.
- **One page per language:** English at `/`, German at `/de/`. The Impressum and Privacy policy are `<details>` sections at the foot of each page with ids `impressum` and `privacy`; a link to `#impressum` or `#privacy` opens the right one, so they can be linked directly. Each page has `hreflang` alternates (en, de, x-default to English), its own canonical URL, title, description and Open Graph tags. The language switch keeps your place: it links to the same section in the other language. There is no automatic redirect by browser language.
- **Performance:** the scene redraws the sky and ridges only when you scroll or move the pointer. Between those, the canvas keeps its pixels and only the lake is redrawn (at half resolution, scaled up), at 30 frames a second. Drawing pauses while a paper panel covers the whole screen and when the tab is hidden. The canvas resolution is capped at 1.5x. Lighthouse reports CLS 0.
- **Security:** strict meta CSP with no `'unsafe-inline'`; scripts set CSS custom properties through the CSSOM, which the CSP allows. Referrer policy `strict-origin-when-cross-origin` (needed for Formspree's domain restriction).
- **Contact form:** works without JavaScript (posts to Formspree directly) and with it (inline validation, AJAX submit, success and error states, focus management, `aria-invalid` and linked error messages). The honeypot uses Formspree's `_gotcha` field. An empty subject is filled with "New enquiry via alfine.ch" / "Neue Anfrage über alfine.ch", and a hidden `language` field tells you which page it came from.
- **Email address:** written as HTML entities in a normal `mailto:` link. It reads and works normally for people and screen readers, without JavaScript, while defeating naive harvesters.
- **404:** one bilingual page, since GitHub Pages serves a single `404.html`.

## Assumptions

- The company is organised under Swiss law with its seat in Zug and is entered in the Zug commercial register, as the Impressum says. Adjust if not.
- No personal names appear anywhere, including the Impressum. Swiss law requires the provider's identity and address; naming authorised signatories is common but optional, so it is left out. Add it to the content files if you want it.
- The legal texts are a careful starting point, not legal advice. They name GitHub (hosting) and Formspree (form) as processors, both in the USA, and leave the email provider as a placeholder.
- The JSON-LD Organization includes name, URL, email, locality (Zug) and country (CH) as requested, plus a one-line translated description so the German page carries German structured data. Remove `description` in `src/templates/layout.mjs` if you want the strict minimum.
- "Our approach" in the hero links to the "Who we are" section.
- The scene shows the view south from Zug's lakefront as an artistic reading of real terrain. Bearings and heights are true; the vertical scale is exaggerated a little (1.6x on wide screens, 2.2x on phones) so the far Alps still read, the framing is about 64 degrees across (34 on a phone), and the houses, forest and steamer are stylised. The sun sets where it does in early winter, beside Pilatus.
- Terrain data: Mapzen Terrain Tiles via the AWS Open Data registry, which include EU-DEM (Copernicus). Their attribution is in the Impressum in both languages.
- The About section has three short pillars (Family-owned, Independent, Rooted in Zug) to give the section structure without inventing facts or figures.
- The principles are the six suggested in the brief, each in one line.
- The mailbox recommendation is Namecheap Private Email for simplicity; Infomaniak is named as the Swiss-hosted alternative.
- GitHub Actions uses the action versions from GitHub's current official Pages example (`checkout@v6`, `upload-pages-artifact@v4`, `deploy-pages@v4`). Newer major versions exist and can be adopted later.
- The build uses the Node.js preinstalled on GitHub's Ubuntu runners (Node 20 or later), so no setup step is needed.
